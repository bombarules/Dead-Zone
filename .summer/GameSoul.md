# Bombies: Dead Zone

## Vision

A squad-based shooter set in a world overrun by mutating creatures called Bombies, where every round is a slow-burn escalation from manageable chaos to full collapse. You cycle between squad members, each with their own tools and range, pushing through industrial ruins while the horde adapts around you.

Playing it feels like holding a line that keeps moving. The tension is not whether the Bombies come, but how fast they change, and whether your squad's mix of traps, gunfire, and positioning can keep up with what they become by round three.

## Genre and Style

- **Type**: First and third person shooter with wave-based zombie survival, squad switching, and room-by-room progression through destructible, multi-location environments.
- **Inspirations**: Left 4 Dead for the horde rhythm and squad dependency; Half-Life 2 for environmental storytelling and gritty industrial weight; DOOM 2016 for the pressure of forward momentum and weapon feel; Resident Evil for dread pacing and special encounter design; Gears of War for the cover logic and the shift between third and first person framing.
- **What makes it theirs**: The Bombies evolve mid-run, not between levels, which means the squad that worked in the first room may need to rotate out before the third. The hazmat engineer's trap grid and the ability to switch who you control turns positioning into a live puzzle rather than a static loadout choice.

## Look and Feel

- **Art style**: Gritty photoreal 3D with worn concrete, rusted metal, and surfaces that look like they have been wet and drying for years. The palette is desaturated charcoal grey and olive green, broken by neon emergency lighting in amber, red, and acid yellow. Volumetric haze, flickering practicals, and harsh directional shadows keep visibility tight and deliberate.
- **Mood and sound**: Quiet moments are near-silent except for dripping water, distant groaning, and radio static. Intense moments hit with low industrial percussion, distorted bass, and the specific wet sound of Bombies moving fast through shallow water. Music builds with the round timer, not with a scripted cue.

## The Hero, the World, the Moment

The rogue hazmat engineer holds the squad's perimeter together from a distance. She deploys proximity traps and lobs toxic grenades that create area-denial zones, letting the close-range squad members funnel Bombies through kill corridors she controls. Switching to her mid-fight changes the whole tempo.

The flooded underground parking garage is the game's opening location: shallow standing water that slows the player just enough to matter, while fast Bombies close distance without the same penalty. Pillars, vehicles, and ramps break sightlines, but the water makes flanking routes visible as splashing movement in the dark.

The collapsed factory sequence is the game's forward-only promise: doors seal behind you, rooms unlock ahead, and the squad must clear each space before the next one opens. No retreating, no replaying a corridor. If someone goes down, you push with what you have left.

## Core Mechanics

The player controls one squad member at a time and switches between them at checkpoints or when one is downed. Bombies come in waves that grow faster and more varied as rounds progress, mixing slow tanks, sprinting runners, and special infected with distinct attack patterns. Weapons span real firearms and experimental tools. The goal is to survive to the end of a location and move to the next. You lose when all squad members are downed simultaneously. Waves scale in density and mutation rate based on time elapsed, not kills. **Assumed**: each location has three to four rounds before a boss-type Bombie triggers the exit sequence.

## First Playable Version

One squad member (the hazmat engineer) in the flooded parking garage. Three rounds of Bombies with basic mutation scaling. Two trap types and one grenade. First and third person toggle. Win state at round three, lose state when downed. Sound, lighting, and water effect before final art pass.

## Open Questions

- Can squad members be permanently lost, or do they recover between locations?
- Do Bombies mutate visually mid-round, or only between rounds?
- Is there friendly fire between squad members?
- What triggers the special infected to appear: a timer, a kill count, or a random roll?
